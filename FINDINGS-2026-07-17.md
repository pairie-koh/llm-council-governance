# Phase 2 Findings: Updating vs Herding on HLE

2026-07-17. For Andy Hall and Rohit Krishnan. Repo `pairie-koh/llm-council-governance`, branch `frontier-rerun`. Every number below reproduces offline from committed JSON with the three commands in the appendix.

## 1. TL;DR

Majority voting over a frontier council is destructive: on the questions where members disagree, the jury scores 38.5% while the best member alone scores 66.2%, and it rescued 0 of 35 lone-correct dissents. Deliberation transfers accuracy downhill: the identical council dragged its strongest member from 66.2% to 59.2% when it chaired, and lifted a weaker chairman from 41.1% to 51.5%. More structure means less damage (court 60.0% > cabinet 59.2% > peer review 52.3% > Opus cabinet 51.5% > jury 38.5%), but no structure beat the best member. One member (Fable 5) dominates this council (60.4% full-pool vs 43.0-48.1% for everyone else, lone-correct on 21 of 35 minority cells), so the current best policy on this benchmark is "ask Fable and ignore the council". The council's one real product is a signal, not an answer: unanimity predicts 67% correctness and disagreement predicts hard questions, except in Physics and Engineering where unanimity is anti-informative (47% (n=17) and 12% (n=16) correct when unanimous).

## 2. Setup

**Council v2** is the strongest model per vendor as of July 2026, all via OpenRouter except Anthropic models, which route direct on research credits:

| Role | Model |
|------|-------|
| Member + chairman | Claude Fable 5 (`anthropic/claude-fable-5`) |
| Member | GPT-5.6 Sol (`openai/gpt-5.6-sol`) |
| Member | Gemini 3.1 Pro (`google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview`) |
| Member | Grok 4.5 (`x-ai/grok-4.5`) |
| Side-arm, not a member | Claude Opus 4.8 (`anthropic/claude-opus-4.8`) |

**Benchmark**: Humanity's Last Exam, text-only multiple choice (560 questions). Biology/Medicine and Chemistry were excluded before any council ran, because the scout showed Fable's safety classifiers refuse 67-71% of questions in those two categories versus 0-11% elsewhere, and Fable is the chairman. That leaves a **374-question eligible pool**. Refusals are recorded as their own outcome and count against accuracy; they are never retried on a fallback model.

**Stage 1** (`run_phase2_stage1.py`): one independent answer per (model, question), 5 models x 374 questions. This is the solo baseline layer. 284 questions are "clean" (all four members produced a scoreable letter). Of those, **154 are unanimous (103 correct) and 130 are disagreement questions**; 35 of the 130 are minority-correct cells (exactly one member right).

**Five aggregation structures** (`run_council_types.py`), all evaluated on the same 130 disagreement questions:

| Structure | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| jury | Offline plurality vote over the four stage-1 letters. Ties are recorded as ties and count as incorrect. |
| cabinet | One Fable 5 chairman call: reads the four answers plus reasoning excerpts, anonymized as Councilor A-D (per-question seeded shuffle), picks a letter. |
| cabinet_opus | Identical mechanism, chaired by Opus 4.8, a chairman weaker than the council's best member. |
| court | One advocate brief per distinct proposed letter (advocates are the non-Anthropic members, round-robin, so no Fable advocate argues before the Fable judge); Fable 5 judges from the briefs. |
| peer_review | Each member anonymously ranks all four answers (its own included, unmarked); Borda count decides. Unparseable ballots are dropped; cross-letter Borda ties count as incorrect. |

**The design is paired.** Every structure re-aggregates the same frozen stage-1 answers, so all comparisons below are within-question: same 130 questions, same four member answers, only the aggregation rule changes. The baseline is solo Fable **on the same 130 questions: 86/130 = 66.2%**. Unanimous questions are decided identically by every structure, so only the disagreement cells discriminate.

## 3. Finding 1: majority voting is destructive

The jury scored **50/130 = 38.5%** against solo Fable's **66.2%** on the same questions. It rescued **0 of 35** minority-correct cells, which is forced by arithmetic: a lone-correct member never holds a plurality. 25 of 130 cells (19%) were ties, all counted incorrect; even scoring ties as coin flips only moves the jury to roughly 48%, still 18 points under the best member.

The mechanism is correlated errors, and it is measured, not assumed. Pairwise phi on correct/wrong outcomes across the full 374-question stage-1 pool:

| Pair | phi | Same wrong letter, given both wrong |
|------|-----|-------------------------------------|
| Fable x Opus (intra-vendor) | +0.63 | 91/104 (88%) |
| Fable x Gemini | +0.64 | 70/87 (80%) |
| Fable x GPT | +0.58 | 75/101 (74%) |
| Fable x Grok | +0.61 | 88/106 (83%) |
| Gemini x GPT | +0.60 | 88/115 (77%) |
| Gemini x Grok | +0.63 | 93/121 (77%) |
| GPT x Grok | +0.56 | 108/143 (76%) |
| Opus x Gemini | +0.63 | 93/114 (82%) |
| Opus x GPT | +0.53 | 101/134 (75%) |
| Opus x Grok | +0.62 | 120/146 (82%) |

The Condorcet jury argument needs independent errors. Every pair here is phi +0.53 to +0.64, and when two models are wrong together they pick the same wrong letter three quarters of the time or more. Under those conditions a vote does not average out noise; it launders the shared error into a confident majority. Concrete case, qid `hle_66f86bbb27a30cecdc2d6c7e` (laser-driven proton acceleration with orbital-angular-momentum photons): Fable alone answered A (correct), reasoning that a vortex beam's ring-shaped intensity profile improves collimation while lowering peak on-axis intensity, so "the peak proton energy decreases... energy is traded for beam quality/collimation." GPT, Gemini, and Grok all answered B. Jury verdict: B, wrong, 3-1.

## 4. Finding 2: deliberation transfers accuracy downhill

This is the headline. The same council, the same 130 questions, the same anonymized answer packets, one variable changed, the chairman:

| Chairman | Chairman solo on subset | As cabinet chairman | Delta |
|----------|------------------------|---------------------|-------|
| Fable 5 (strongest member) | 66.2% (86/130) | 59.2% (77/130) | -7.0pp |
| Opus 4.8 (weaker than best member) | 41.1% (53/129 clean; 40.8% if its one no_answer counts against) | 51.5% (67/130) | +10.4pp |

Reading the council lifts a weak chairman and drags a strong one down. Deliberation regresses the decider toward the council mean; it moves accuracy from wherever it is concentrated to wherever it is not. The Opus lift is individually significant in the paired flip accounting (21 questions Opus-solo-wrong to chair-right vs 7 the other way, exact binomial p = 0.013); the Fable drop is 14 right-to-wrong vs 5 wrong-to-right (p = 0.064), and the chairman-identity contrast itself (Fable chair vs Opus chair, 19 vs 9 discordant questions) has p = 0.087. Direction is consistent everywhere; individual significance is noted per cell.

The rescue/capitulation asymmetry explains where Fable's 7 points went. The cabinet did what the jury cannot: it rescued **16/35 (45.7%)** lone-correct cells, because a chairman reading arguments can side with one voice against three. But it paid for those rescues with more capitulations: on 14 questions where Fable's own stage-1 answer was right, the cabinet answer came out wrong. Anonymization makes this vivid. On qid `hle_66f8f56e204cc74a525a23b8` (Kenneth Burke's "Tribal No"), Fable's stage-1 answer E was the lone correct one; as chairman it faced its own answer relabeled "Councilor D" against three councilors on A, and ruled: "Councilor D's argument, while sophisticated, misapplies Burke's idea/image distinction to the wrong negative. The majority reasoning better fits Burke's hortatory-first account... FINAL ANSWER: A." The chairman talked itself out of its own right answer. The reverse failure also occurs: on `hle_6739691ecea30e65e1f96876` (reward range for geometric convergence of value iteration), three members including Fable had the right answer E and Grok alone argued A; the chairman found the lone dissenter's boundedness argument persuasive and ruled A, flipping off a correct 3-1 majority that included its own answer.

## 5. Finding 3: structure ranking

More structure means less damage, and nothing beats the best member:

| Structure | Disagreement accuracy (n=130) | Rescue rate (n=35) | Ties / no_answer | New OpenRouter cost |
|-----------|-------------------------------|--------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| solo Fable (baseline) | 66.2% (86/130) | 21/35 by definition of its lone-correct cells | 0 | $0 |
| court | 60.0% (78/130) | 16/35 | 3 no_answer | $8.31 |
| cabinet | 59.2% (77/130) | 16/35 | 0 | $0 |
| peer_review | 52.3% (68/130) | 5/35 | 4 ties | $24.19 |
| cabinet_opus | 51.5% (67/130) | 9/35 | 0 | $0 |
| jury | 38.5% (50/130) | 0/35 | 25 ties | $0 |

The ordering tracks how much of the members' reasoning the mechanism actually uses. The jury sees only letters and destroys 27.7 points relative to solo Fable. Peer review sees reasoning but aggregates by rank ballots, which is vote-like, and its Borda count buried lone-correct answers (5/35 rescues). The cabinet reads arguments and recovers most of the jury's loss. The court, which forces one explicit brief per candidate answer and lets a judge compare them, is the best aggregation tested and recovers the most herding loss (its 3 no_answers are counted against it; on answered questions it is 78/127 = 61.4%). Pool-level, accepting the unanimous letter on unanimous questions: court 63.7%, cabinet 63.4%, vs Fable's 67% on the same 284-question clean pool. Adversarial advocacy is the right direction; it still does not reach the best member.

## 6. Finding 4: one dominant member, and what that implies

Fable 5 dominates this council. Full 374-question pool, refusals and non-answers counted against:

| Model | Accuracy (n=374) |
|-------|------------------|
| Claude Fable 5 | 60.4% (13 refusals, 21 no_answer counted against) |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | 48.1% |
| Claude Opus 4.8 (side-arm) | 46.8% |
| Grok 4.5 | 45.2% |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro | 43.0% (70 no_answer counted against) |

On the disagreement subset the gap widens: Fable 66.2%, Gemini 38.5%, GPT 33.8%, Grok 27.7%. Fable is the lone-correct dissenter on **21 of the 35** minority-correct cells; the other three members combined account for 14.

The implication cuts two ways. First, in a one-dominant-member regime, every aggregation rule is downside: the information the council could add is mostly already inside its best member, so any transfer mechanism mostly transfers error inward. "Ask Fable, ignore the council" beats every structure tested here. Second, this is a regime statement, not a universal one. Phase 1's small-model tier had near-equal members (71.7/62.7/60.4/44.0) and there four of seven structures beat the best member. The council-helps result and the council-hurts result are both real; the regime variable is the accuracy spread between the best member and the rest, plus the error correlation. Note also that the pre-registered bio/chem exclusion removes exactly the categories where Fable refuses, so the eligible pool is tilted toward Fable's strengths; see section 8.

## 7. Finding 5: disagreement is the useful signal

The council's vote is a bad answer but a decent confidence meter. On the 284-question clean pool, when all four members agree they are right **103/154 = 67%**; the disagreement subset is where accuracy collapses for every mechanism (38.5-66.2%). Unanimity-as-accept, disagreement-as-escalate is a routing signal you get for free from independent sampling, no deliberation needed.

Per-category (`analyze_query_types`), clean questions only:

| Category | n | Disagree rate | Unanimity acc | Fable | Fable on disagr. | jury | cabinet | court |
|----------|---|---------------|---------------|-------|------------------|------|---------|-------|
| Humanities/Soc Sci | 75 | 56% | 76% | 77% | 79% | 48% | 71% | 67% |
| Math | 65 | 31% | 84% | 75% | 55% (n=20) | 35% | 60% (n=20) | 55% |
| CS/AI | 56 | 48% | 79% | 75% | 70% | 37% | 59% | 63% |
| Other | 37 | 62% | 50% | 51% | 52% | 35% | 39% | 57% |
| Physics | 30 | 43% | 47% (n=17) | 50% | 54% | 8% | 46% | 38% |
| Engineering | 21 | 24% | 12% (n=16) | 29% | 80% (n=5) | 80% | 80% | 80% |

Two cells qualify the headline signal. **Physics and Engineering break unanimity-as-accept**: unanimous councils are right only 47% (n=17) and 12% (n=16) of the time there. In Engineering the signal inverts outright: unanimous consensus is nearly always wrong, while on its 5 disagreement cells every structure and Fable hit 80% (n=5). Confident shared error concentrates in exactly the categories where plausible-but-wrong quantitative consensus is easiest. **Math is the only cell where any structure edges solo Fable**: cabinet 60% vs Fable 55%, but that is 12 vs 11 correct on n=20, one question, and carries no weight.

## 8. What this does not show

- **One benchmark family.** Everything here is verifiable multiple choice with a single right letter. Councils for open-ended judgment, synthesis, or policy compliance are untested by this design; the phase-1 pilot already showed MCQ results do not automatically transfer.
- **One regime.** Council v2 has a single dominant member. The findings say aggregation is harmful *in that regime*; phase 1 showed the opposite in a near-equals regime. Do not quote "councils hurt" without the regime qualifier.
- **The pool is tilted toward the dominant member.** Bio/chem were excluded because Fable refuses there. That was pre-registered and necessary (Fable chairs), but it means Fable's 60.4% and its 21/35 lone-correct share are measured on a pool curated around its refusal behavior.
- **Clean-pool attrition is not random.** 284 of 374 questions are clean; the drops are mostly Gemini's 70 no_answers and Fable's 13 refusals plus 21 no_answers, which could bias the composition of the disagreement subset.
- **n = 130, single run, no seed replicates.** Which gaps survive n=130, using exact McNemar on paired discordant counts: jury vs solo Fable is decisive (0 vs 36 discordant questions, p = 3e-11). Peer review and cabinet_opus vs solo Fable are solid (p = 0.0005 both). Cabinet vs solo Fable (p = 0.064) and court vs solo Fable (p = 0.115) are directionally consistent but not individually significant. **Court vs cabinet is 78 vs 77 correct, discordant 10 vs 9, p = 1.0: the court > cabinet ordering is not established.** Court vs peer_review and Fable-chair vs Opus-chair are both p = 0.087, suggestive only. The five-structure ranking's endpoints are real; the middle ordering is not.
- **Category cells are small.** Engineering unanimity 12% is n=16; the Math cabinet edge is n=20; Physics unanimity is n=17. These are leads, not results.

## 9. What's next

Arms A/B/D (`run_phase2_arms.py`, built, not yet run) decompose *why* deliberation moves accuracy downhill, at the member level on the same 130 questions with the same paired design: Arm A shows each member the peers' anonymized answers with reasoning and allows revision; Arm B is identical but positions-only (letters, no arguments), so A-vs-B separates persuasion-by-argument from herding-by-vote-count; Arm D is Rohit's council-as-armor probe, injecting an evidence-free adversarial challenge against each member's answer, alone (D1) versus with peers' positions visible (D2), so D2-vs-D1 measures whether peer presence hardens a member against content-free pressure. After that: Rohit's compliance cases, extending the updating-vs-herding measurement past verifiable MCQ into policy-judgment territory where "correct" is defined by a spec; and the harness direction, operationalizing finding 5 as a router (accept unanimity, escalate disagreement to the best solo model or a court, with the Physics/Engineering unanimity trap handled per category).

## 10. Reproduction appendix

All three commands read committed local JSON, make no API calls, and cost nothing. From the repo root (`pairie-koh/llm-council-governance`, branch `frontier-rerun`):

```
python -m experiments.run_phase2_stage1 --analyze-only   # stage-1: 374 HLE questions x 5 models
python -m experiments.run_council_types --analyze-only   # 5 structures on the 130-question disagreement subset
python -m experiments.analyze_query_types                # per-category slicing
```

Structure mechanisms are specified in the docstrings of `experiments/run_council_types.py`; per-question records with full chairman/judge/advocate transcripts are in `experiments/results_council_types_v2/council_types_results.json` (qids quoted above are directly greppable there).

Spend: stage 1 cost **$79.73** in new OpenRouter spend (the stage-1 analyzer's $104.12 figure is total recorded call cost, which includes the scout-seeded records and the notional cost of Anthropic-direct calls); council types cost **$32.50** OpenRouter ($8.31 court advocates + $24.19 peer-review ballots; jury, cabinet, and cabinet_opus were $0 because plurality is offline and both chairmen are Anthropic-direct). All Anthropic-side calls ran on research credits at $0 to the study budget.
